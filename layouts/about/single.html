About

The New India Foundation

The New India Foundation, based in Bengaluru, matches public-spirited philanthropy with relevant scholarship.

In the seven-and-a-half decades since Independence, there has been a large body of work produced by Indian historians and social scientists. Taken singly, many of these studies are impressive; viewed cumulatively, they add up to much less than what one might expect.

The chief reason for this is the determining influence on scholarly practice of one single date: 15 August 1947. Historians do not look beyond the attainment of Independence, whereas other social scientists do not tend to look back at all.

Given India's size, its importance, and its interest, and given that this is our country, the lack of good research on its modern history is unfortunate.

It is this lack that the New India Foundation seeks to address, by sponsoring high-quality original scholarship on different aspects of independent India. Its activities include the granting of NIF Book Fellowships for highly researched original work, Translation Fellowships for bringing historical Indian-language works into English, the NIF Book Prize, and the Girish Karnad Memorial Lecture.

In memoriam

The Girish Karnad Memorial NIF Lecture

An annual public lecture delivered by a leading intellectual on themes around modern India, instituted in memory of Girish Karnad (1938–2019) — playwright, actor, film-maker and Jnanpith laureate, whose plays Tughlaq, Hayavadana and Nagamandala reshaped the Indian stage, and who lived and worked in Bengaluru for much of his life.

Details of the next lecture will be announced here and to subscribers.

Partnership

Bhashavaad

NIF collaborates with the Ashoka Centre for Translation on Bhashavaad, an annual conference on translation in India. The conference has also produced a searchable database of translations into and between Indian languages, now running to more than thirty thousand entries.

Visit bhashavaad.in
