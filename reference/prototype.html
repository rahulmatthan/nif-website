About

The New India Foundation

The New India Foundation, based in Bengaluru, matches public-spirited philanthropy with relevant scholarship.

In the seven-and-a-half decades since Independence, there has been a large body of work produced by Indian historians and social scientists. Taken singly, many of these studies are impressive; viewed cumulatively, they add up to much less than what one might expect.

The chief reason for this is the determining influence on scholarly practice of one single date: 15 August 1947. Historians do not look beyond the attainment of Independence, whereas other social scientists do not tend to look back at all.

Given India's size, its importance, and its interest, and given that this is our country, the lack of good research on its modern history is unfortunate.

It is this lack that the New India Foundation seeks to address, by sponsoring high-quality original scholarship on different aspects of independent India. Its activities include the granting of NIF Book Fellowships for highly researched original work, Translation Fellowships for bringing historical Indian-language works into English, the NIF Book Prize, and the Girish Karnad Memorial Lecture.

Avantha Chair, King's India Institute

Avantha Chair at King's India Institute, King's College London; Centennial Professor in the Department of Gender Studies at the London School of Economics; formerly Professor at the Centre for the Study of Law and Governance, JNU. She delivered the Radhakrishnan Memorial Lectures at All Souls College, Oxford in 2009 and was Vice-President of the American Political Science Association, 2011–12.

Professor of History and International Relations at Ashoka University and Senior Fellow at Carnegie India. He took his PhD in War Studies at King's College London, where he taught for many years, and before entering academia spent six years as an infantry officer in the Indian Army. His books include War and Peace in Modern India (2010), 1971: A Global History of the Creation of Bangladesh (2013), India's War (2016) and Fierce Enigmas (2018). He has served on the National Security Advisory Board and as Chief Editor of the Kargil War History for the Ministry of Defence. Recipient of the K. Managing Trustee; Vice Chairman, TeamLease Services

Co-founder and Vice Chairman of TeamLease Services, India's largest staffing and human capital firm, which also operates India's first vocational university and a national PPP apprenticeship programme. Co-founder and Non-Executive Chairman of Infosys, and Co-founder and Chairman of EkStep. He was Chairman of the Unique Identification Authority of India in the rank of a Cabinet Minister. Book Fellowships · since 2004

NIF Book Fellowships

The core activity of the New India Foundation, awarded annually to scholars and writers working on different aspects of the history of independent India. Fellows may write a memoir, a work of reportage, or a thickly footnoted academic study; the books may be oriented towards economics, politics or culture.

From several hundred applications, roughly fifteen to twenty proposals are shortlisted. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed by a jury drawn from the Foundation's trustees alongside eminent figures from scholarship, business and public life.

The books that result convey original research in an accessible manner, and each is published by an established house. Beyond the stipend, the Fellowship provides editorial, legal and administrative guidance from proposal to publication, introductions to publishers and agents, and both online and offline community support.

What the fellowship is worth ₹18,00,000 in total — ₹1,00,000 a month for twelve months, with the balance of ₹6,00,000 payable on submission of the final manuscript. Open to Indian nationals, including those currently living abroad.

Translation Fellowships

NIF Translation Fellowships

Instituted to bring significant non-fiction written in Indian languages into English. Some of the most important books about India have been written in Indian languages, and many of them remain inaccessible to readers outside their linguistic region.

The Fellowship awards a six-month grant of ₹6,00,000 to translate a non-fiction work originally written in one of ten Indian languages: Assamese, Bangla, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Tamil and Urdu.

Applicants should be fluent in the source language and in English, and should have prior experience of translation. Selection turns on the significance of the proposed text and the translator's ability to render it for a contemporary readership. The Fellowship is open equally to established translators and to debutants. The jury comprises the NIF Trustees alongside a Language Expert Committee covering all ten languages, made up of bilingual scholars, professors and literary translators.

Recipients are expected to commit full time to the translation for its duration. The fellowship is non-renewable.

Applying

What makes a strong application

An original question

The Foundation looks for topics that have not already been worked over, and for a question the applicant is genuinely trying to answer rather than a subject they intend to survey.

Evidence of depth

Show the archive, the fieldwork or the interview access the book will rest on, and show that you have already begun. Deep reporting distinguishes almost every book the Fellowship has produced.

A realistic year

The Fellowship runs for twelve months and expects a completed manuscript at the end of it. Applications that account honestly for scope and time carry more weight than ambitious ones that do not.

Applicants may be debut writers or much-published authors; the Foundation looks for a distinctive voice, an original idea, and a serious commitment to finishing.

Questions

Fellowship FAQs

What is the Book Fellowship, and what do you look for?
The Book Fellowship allows fellows to write on any aspect of modern India. From several hundred applications the most compelling proposals are selected. The Foundation looks for original topics, deep research, and relevance to modern India. Applicants may be debut writers or much-published authors — what matters is a unique voice, an original idea, and a commitment to completing a manuscript within the term.
What is the Translation Fellowship?
It brings historical and significant Indian-language non-fiction into English. The Foundation looks for applicants adept in both the source language and English, with prior translation experience. Selection turns on the relevance of the proposed text and the translator's ability to render it for a contemporary readership. Like the Book Fellowship, it is open to established translators and debutants alike.
How much is the Book Fellowship worth?
₹18,00,000 in total: ₹1,00,000 a month for twelve months, plus ₹6,00,000 on submission of the final manuscript.
How much is the Translation Fellowship worth?
₹6,00,000 over six months.
Who can apply?
The Book Fellowship is open to Indian nationals, including those currently living abroad. The Translation Fellowship is open to translators working from Assamese, Bangla, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Tamil or Urdu into English.
How often is the Fellowship awarded?
The Book Fellowship is awarded annually. The next call opens in August 2026, with submissions closing in November 2026 and fellowships beginning in August 2027.
What support is offered beyond the grant?
Editorial, legal and administrative guidance from proposal through to publication; introductions to publishers and agents; mentorship; and access to the community of past and present fellows.
Can a fellowship book be entered for the Book Prize?
No. Books that have been supported by a Foundation fellowship are not eligible for the NIF Book Prize.
How are applications assessed?
The Trustees assess all proposals and draw up a shortlist. Shortlisted candidates are interviewed in person by a jury comprising the Trustees alongside eminent figures from scholarship, business and public life. For translation projects the jury also includes the Language Expert Committee for the relevant language.

Process

The jury

The prize is judged by the Foundation's Governing Board — Niraja Gopal Jayal, who chairs the Book Prize, together with Srinath Raghavan, Manish Sabharwal and Rahul Matthan, with Nandan Nilekani as Emeritus Board Member.

The jury reads towards a longlist announced in September, a shortlist in October, and a winner in November. Where a submission requires specialist knowledge — a translated work, or a subject outside the Board's own fields — external readers are brought in to advise.

Read the Board's biographies

Eligibility

What can be entered

  • Any nationality.There is no restriction on where the writer is from or lives.
  • Non-fiction only, any genre.History, biography, political commentary, travel, memoir, reportage — anything that offers insight into the making of modern India.
  • English or translated into English.Books originally written in an Indian language and translated into English are eligible, and have won.
  • Professionally published.Books must have national or international distribution. Self-published works are not eligible.
  • Published in the preceding year.The 2026 prize considers books published in 2025.
  • Not an NIF fellowship book.Books supported by a Foundation fellowship are not eligible for the prize.

Questions

Book Prize FAQs

How is a book nominated?
Publishers and literary agents may nominate titles from November. From January, authors may also submit their own books. The Foundation's team and jury additionally consider eligible books on their own initiative, so a book need not be formally nominated to be read. Nominations for the 2026 prize close on 31 January 2026. Write to submissions@newindiafoundation.org.
What is the prize money?
The winner receives ₹15,00,000. Where the winning book is a translation, the award is shared between author and translator.
When was the prize established?
In 2018. The first prize was awarded that year to Milan Vaishnav for When Crime Pays.
Are translated works considered?
Yes. Books originally written in Indian languages and translated into English are eligible. Shekhar Pathak's The Chipko Movement, translated from the Hindi by Manisha Chaudhry, won in 2022.
Are self-published books or e-books eligible?
Only professionally published books with national or international distribution are considered. Self-published works are not eligible.
What genres are eligible?
There is no limitation within non-fiction. History, biography, political commentary, travelogue, memoir, reportage and academic monographs have all been longlisted or have won.
Who judges the prize?
The Governing Board of the New India Foundation, chaired for the prize by Niraja Gopal Jayal, with external specialist readers brought in where a submission requires them.
Is there a fee to enter?
No. There is no entry fee for nomination by a publisher, an agent or an author.
